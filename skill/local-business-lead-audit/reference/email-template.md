# Outreach templates

All drafts wait for the user's approval. The user sends them from their own mailbox.

## First email

```
Subject: <domain> <the single strongest verified problem>
         e.g. "example-clinic.com takes about 8 seconds to show on a phone"

Hello <Business> team,

I ran <domain> through Google's PageSpeed test for phones on <weekday, date>.
<Finding 1, with a number>. <Why it costs them customers, in their terms>.

Two other things from the same visit: <finding 2>, and <finding 3>.

I run <sender business>, a <city> <what you do>. I can send you the full report
with the specific fixes at no cost. If you want help carrying them out, we can
talk after you've seen it.

Shall I send it over?

If you'd prefer not to hear from me again, reply "stop" and I won't write again.
<signature>
```

Rules:
- Dated measurements, never "today".
- Use shares for anything that depends on screen size ("about half the images"), not exact counts.
- Every claim is re-checked on the live site right before sending.
- No prices, no discounts, no invented deadlines.
- One question at the end.

## Follow-up (5–7 days later, same thread, only if they haven't replied)

Reply in the same thread ("Re: <first subject>"). Open on the gap between what their customers want and what's in the way, teach one useful thing, and end with one question. Three to five sentences.

## Call opener (for businesses already emailed)

1. "Hi, this is <name> from <business>. I emailed on <date> about your website. Could I speak to whoever looks after your marketing? It'll take two minutes."
2. Name one problem: "When I checked <site> on a phone, <problem>. Most people searching on their phone leave before that. Have you noticed?"
3. Ask, then listen: "Where do most of your new customers come from right now?"
4. One offer: "Can I send you the short report with the fixes? It's free."
5. "Not now" or "no": thank them. Anyone who asks not to be contacted goes on the do-not-contact list.

## Opener for businesses with no website but strong reviews

"Hi, this is <name> from <business>. I found <business> on Google Maps: <rating> stars from <n> reviews, which is excellent. I couldn't find a website or social pages, so someone searching for <a dentist> in <town> only sees a pin on the map. Where do most of your new <patients> come from right now?"
